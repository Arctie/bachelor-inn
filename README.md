# Noble Nights: A Project Postmortem

## Scope and purpose

Noble Nights received recognition at the Norwegian Game Awards. Despite this achievement, the original development team subsequently fragmented, and the project approached shutdown before later being revived.

This postmortem examines the development phase in which I participated. Its purpose is to understand the factors that contributed to the team's difficulties, identify lessons for future game-development projects, and document practices that might help other teams avoid similar outcomes.

The analysis will consider organizational structure, decision-making, communication, technical processes, project planning, and the relationship between individual responsibilities and collective goals.

I intend to base the investigation on available evidence, including project history, technical records, written communications, and recollections of the people involved. I will distinguish documented events from personal interpretations and acknowledge uncertainty where the available evidence is incomplete.

The objective is not to assign blame for its own sake, but to understand what the team expected from the project, and what my expectations were and how it fell short of those expectations.

## Method

A proper postmortem could follow the IEEE standard for postmortems, as described here: [https://courses.cs.umbc.edu/undergraduate/345/spring12/mitchell/readings/aDefinedProcessForProjectPostMortemReview.pdf](https://courses.cs.umbc.edu/undergraduate/345/spring12/mitchell/readings/aDefinedProcessForProjectPostMortemReview.pdf)

I will however make a much simpler one based on the following questions to better understand how the project didn't live up to my expectations:

* What did we set out to build?
* What went well?
* What went wrong?
* Which decisions and events contributed to the outcome?
* What was within my control, and what wasn't?
* What will I do differently next time?
